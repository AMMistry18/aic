Visual and Contact Registration for Last-Inch
SC and SFP Connector Insertion
Working draft for author review
Abstract— Reliable connector insertion requires correcting
the errors that sensors can actually observe near an occluded
mating surface. We extend an existing conﬁdence-weighted mul-
tiview perception pipeline with connector-speciﬁc relative con-
tact registration: an optical connector uses its terminal shoulder,
whereas a small form-factor pluggable module uses opposed
entrance-lip and side-wall measurements. Bounded impedance-
control targets carry the resulting corrections through insertion.
We evaluate the held-grasp, upstream-aligned last-inch task
in headless Gazebo using public challenge geometry and an
independent geometry, contact, and full-rate force recorder.
A frozen partial development snapshot contains six veriﬁed
optical-connector seats in six policy trials and ﬁve veriﬁed
module seats in six policy trials; one additional module setup
failed before execution. All completed policy trials have com-
plete 2,000 Hz force coverage, with a maximum raw tared force
of 3.322 N. A module perception rejection and a controller
startup failure remain in the record. The evidence demonstrates
conditional insertion capability and exposes timing and observ-
ability failures; broader robustness and current-method causal
comparisons remain under evaluation.
Index Terms— Robotic assembly, connector insertion, visual
perception, contact sensing, simulation evaluation.
I. APPLICATION AND SCOPE
SC optical connectors and small form-factor pluggable
(SFP) modules present different mating geometries despite
sharing a familiar align-and-insert task. Near engagement,
the gripper and connector obscure useful image features,
and compliant robot tracking can leave residual errors af-
ter a valid pose command. Our starting point is a chal-
lenge pipeline that combines learned landmarks, conﬁdence-
weighted multiview reconstruction, a visually estimated
grasp transform, and force-aware insertion [1]. We retain that
perception structure and isolate the ﬁnal insertion skill for
inspection in Gazebo.
The task begins with a connector held rigidly near the
requested port. Upstream angular alignment is prescribed;
translation and the in-hand offset are estimated from images.
The policy preserves the handed-off wrist orientation. SC
angular perturbations have components within ±0.1◦; SFP
uses ±0.03◦and a public level-ﬁxture prior. Seeded in-
hand translation can vary by ±2 mm per component. These
conditions deﬁne conditional last-inch insertion, excluding
pickup, long-range transport, unrestricted rotational recovery,
and release retention.
Related work already establishes the main ingredients.
Active Extrinsic Contact Sensing estimates external contacts
to guide insertion [2]. Insert-One combines visual tracking
with impedance control under in-hand pose uncertainty [3].
SyPAS uses synthetic socket perception and visual servoing

port keypoints [1]. Conﬁdence-weighted triangulation and

and shape checks reject inconsistent measurements. Detector
conﬁdence supplies ﬁtting weights, not a calibrated proba-
bility of pose accuracy. The original SC plug checkpoint is
retained. The SC mouth and SFP plug/network-interface-card
models are adapted from their original checkpoints using 20
rendered training scenes and six separate development scenes

B. Geometry determines the physical datum

axis a, the controller uses relative depth bd = a⊤(bpP −bpM).
A visual depth bias can persist even when the robot tracks its
command. A physical datum registers this relative coordinate
through a separate measurement.

C. Bounded execution
The estimated tip target is converted to a TCP command
using Eq. 1. Cartesian impedance, persistent depth setpoints,
bounded lateral proportional-integral compensation, and ter-
minal braking reduce tracking error. Compensation integrates
own-robot error, freezes during contact measurements, and
has bounded authority. The evaluated SFP proﬁle requests 10
mm/s travel and 2 mm/s near the seat, with a 3 N travel pause
and 6 N inner abort. The separate 20 N measured resultant
ceiling is veriﬁed from raw data; a lower-rate pre-command
guard cannot guarantee that bound. The faster development
candidate is not included in the reported cohort.
III. TESTBED AND INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION
The scene uses public Phase 1 assets [8], pinned at revision
7148df9: ﬁve network-interface cards with two SFP slots
each, ﬁve SC duplex mounts, staging ﬁxtures, source cables,
a UR5e arm, and an ATI force/torque sensor. Original visual
and collision meshes remain active. A declared rigid grasp
attachment replaces upstream manipulation; automatic port
attachment is disabled. A source-audited double-precision
Bullet backend runs at 0.5 ms with 200 solver iterations and
declared cable-joint damping. These are simulation model
choices, not measurements of physical connector compli-
ance.
Before policy execution, a calibrated hold checks reset
accuracy, contact health, and six camera streams. Production
inputs are RGB, camera calibration, exposure-time own-
robot transforms, kinematics, public geometry, and wrist
wrench. Object poses, hidden contact state, and scoring
signals are excluded from the policy and retained for diag-
nostics or independent evaluation only. Setup failures remain
recorded alongside policy failures.

and checks the requested target and healthy contact obser-

contact. Positive-gap cached manifolds with zero solver
impulse do not sufﬁce. SFP’s clearance seat requires both
source-geometry non-overlap and an independently executed
deliberate lateral miss with the identical collision contract,

IV. MEASURED DEVELOPMENT RESULTS
A. Recovered-policy snapshot
We freeze the adapted checkpoint hashes, controller
sources, and measurement proﬁles before replaying prede-
ﬁned development handoffs across target identities. Table II
is an immutable partial snapshot before speed tuning. The
newly trained checkpoints deﬁne this measured cohort; ear-
lier success counts are not pooled with it. Reused develop-
ment cases and shared simulation seeds with distinct grasp
conditions must not be interpreted as independent held-out
reliability samples.
The SFP attempt denominator includes one controller-
bootstrap timeout and its subsequent policy retry. That retry
rejected a 2.393 px rim residual against the unchanged 2 px
gate before insertion. The SC setup denominator includes
one duplicate setup cancelled before policy execution after
a resume-bookkeeping error. All attempts remain in the
trial ledger. The snapshot has six distinct attempted case
identities per connector; SC includes shared-seed cases with
different grasp conditions. Descriptive Wilson intervals over
completed policy trials are 61.0–100.0% for SC and 43.6–
97.0% for SFP. They quantify small-count uncertainty, not
population robustness. The remaining predeﬁned replay cases
are pending.

B. What failed and which changes helped
An earlier matched SC component screen isolates two
ingredients under the same upstream-aligned condition (Ta-
ble III). On ﬁve ﬁxed development cases, removing image-
based body translation or lateral tracking eliminated veriﬁed
seats. That revision predates current axial registration and the
recovered checkpoints. It supports those components on that
screen; it does not establish the current combined method’s
causal advantage.
Failure analysis also exposed errors outside the contact
controller. In an earlier SFP cohort, three of ten trials
failed before motion because their exposure-time transforms
expired during computation. Retaining copied transforms
before inference repaired all three selected failures and
preserved one earlier pass: 4/4 under unchanged scoring, with
raw peaks 2.9993–3.1833 N. These selected repairs form a
separate cohort and are not pooled into Table II. Likewise,
preserving executed search intent repaired a sampling-gap
failure in probe readiness, but did not relax the sensor
windows needed to accept contact.
The recovered SFP case 45005 took 57.458 simulated and
715.483 wall seconds. Its ﬁnal stroke accounted for only
6.96 simulated seconds; the policy’s travel samples peaked
at 1.412 N and never reached the 3 N pause. Perception,
approach, and registration therefore dominate this exam-
ple’s cycle time. Whole-action raw peaks and these lower-
rate travel observations are distinct measurements. Raising

V. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
Connector geometry determines which physical datum is
available. A terminal shoulder supports SC axial registration;
SFP instead needs an entrance datum and shallow lateral
contacts. The proposed allocation makes those assumptions
and acceptance checks explicit while preserving the origi-
nal learned, conﬁdence-weighted visual initialization. Inde-
pendent veriﬁcation prevents estimated depth or command
completion from being mistaken for a physical seat.
The frozen snapshot contains six veriﬁed SC seats and
ﬁve SFP seats, with one SFP perception rejection, one
startup failure and a cancelled duplicate setup retained. All
completed policy trials satisfy the raw-force coverage audit.
Earlier SC component removals and transform-expiry repairs
motivate the design, while broader causal and reliability
claims remain open.
The study is simulation-only and conditional on a rigid
grasp, upstream angular alignment, and the SFP ﬁxture prior.
It establishes held geometric seating, not electrical mating,
latch retention, cable grasp robustness, or hardware transfer.
[Results pending: current axial-correction and tracking abla-

selection and a source/ checkpoint freeze.] Those trials will
report all-selected denominators, failure causes, conﬁdence
intervals, and separate simulation/wall times.
